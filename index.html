    How it works

    • Systems: LKS-92 / Latvia TM (EPSG:3059) and LKS-2020 / Latvia TM (EPSG:10306). Both use the same Transverse Mercator projection on GRS80: central meridian 24°E, scale 0.9996, false easting 500 000 m, false northing −6 000 000 m.
    • Both directions: X/Y input gets wgs84_lat/wgs84_lon; latitude/longitude input (decimal degrees like 56.951389 or 56,951389, or 56°57'05"N) gets lks_x/lks_y, valid for both LKS-92 and LKS-2020. Which one a file holds is detected from the column names and the values.
    • Axes: X is northing (about 170 000–440 000 in Latvia), Y is easting (about 300 000–770 000). If a file has them the other way round, the converter notices and swaps them.
    • Accuracy: EPSG defines LKS-92 → WGS84 (transformation 1958) and LKS-2020 → WGS84 (10840) as a zero shift accurate to 1 m, so one calculation serves both systems. That's fine for maps, GIS and devices, not for survey work. The projection itself matches PROJ (what QGIS uses) to well under a millimetre.
    • CSV files: separators ; , tab |; numbers like 311980.51, 311980,51 or 311 980,51; UTF-8 (with or without BOM) or Windows-1257. The download keeps the file's separator, encoding and line endings.
    • Check: every point is compared with Latvia's border and coastline (Natural Earth 1:10m, accurate to a few hundred metres): in Latvia (or within 1 km), 1–25 km outside (across the border or at sea), further away, which usually means wrong data. Rows where X and Y look swapped and duplicate coordinates are listed too.
    • Privacy: the page has no server side. Your coordinates and files stay on your computer. The map's background tiles come from OpenStreetMap, which sees which map area is shown.
    Static page, no tracking. Method: Krüger series per C. F. F. Karney, “Transverse Mercator with an accuracy of a few nanometers” (2011).
